# Abstract

Enterprise Instant Messaging (EIM) platforms carry substantial organizational tacit knowledge across daily technical discussions, incident troubleshooting, policy updates, and project coordination. However, EIM message streams exhibit short and colloquial utterances, interleaved topics, heterogeneous multimodal attachments, temporal knowledge evolution, and strict conversation-level access boundaries. Applying conventional retrieval-augmented generation pipelines directly to EIM data leads to fragmented retrieval units, multimodal information loss, and potential permission leakage. To address these challenges under privacy-preserving local deployment constraints, this thesis designs and implements a multimodal knowledge retrieval Agent system for enterprise instant messaging.

For knowledge construction, this thesis proposes a unified Multimodal Knowledge Unit (MKU) representation that converts images (combining OCR and vision-language descriptions), spreadsheets (row-level serialization), documents, and voice messages into indexable semantic units. An online topic segmentation algorithm combining semantic similarity, reply/mention graph structure, and temporal decay is introduced to disentangle interleaved chat streams, supporting summary-enhanced conversation units, context-injected attachment units, and incremental updates. For retrieval, the system integrates dense semantic vectors, BGE-M3 lexical sparse weights, BM25, and Chinese-CLIP cross-modal visual search, followed by weighted reciprocal rank fusion, cross-encoder reranking, and time-decay scoring, while pushing conversation membership access control lists directly into vector filtering alongside secondary verification. For question answering, a LangGraph-based retrieval-planning Agent orchestrates query understanding, hybrid search, sufficiency reflection, citation-constrained generation, and sentence-level evidence verification, returning Markdown responses linked to clickable original message cards.

All models run locally using open-source weights on a standard workstation without discrete GPUs, decoupled from specific messaging platforms via a unified adapter interface and verified through a self-built IM simulator and admin console. Experimental results on the synthetic benchmark SimEIM (946 messages, 24 multimodal attachments, 131 QA pairs) and public Chinese benchmark C-MTEB T2Retrieval demonstrate clear effectiveness: the proposed topic segmentation achieves 0.571 Exact-F1 and 0.848 one-to-one overlap, outperforming time-gap and fixed-window baselines; multi-path fusion with reranking reaches 0.956 Hit@1 on SimEIM and 0.9272 nDCG@10 (0.9150 Recall@10) on T2Retrieval; and the end-to-end Agent improves accuracy over the NaiveRAG baseline across text (0.914 vs. 0.837), document (0.929 vs. 0.893), and multi-hop queries (0.700 vs. 0.600), while raising unanswerable refusal rate to 0.917 with zero unauthorized content disclosure. All ten system functional test cases passed.

**Keywords**: Enterprise Instant Messaging; Multimodal Knowledge Retrieval; Topic Segmentation; Hybrid Retrieval; LLM Agent
